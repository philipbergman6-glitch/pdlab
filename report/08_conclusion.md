# Conclusion

The Prisoner's Dilemma is one game with three very different theories
attached to it, and this report has tried to show how they fit together
by treating each of them both analytically and numerically.

*Rationality.* In the one-shot game defection strictly dominates and
$(D,D)$ is the unique Nash equilibrium (Theorem 2.8); finite repetition
changes nothing (Theorems 3.4 and 3.5); infinite repetition with discount factor
$\delta$ changes everything, but only in the sense of the folk theorems:
cooperation becomes *sustainable*, by Grim Trigger for
$\delta \ge (T-R)/(T-P)$ and by Tit-for-Tat as a Nash equilibrium for
$\delta \ge \max\{(T-R)/(T-P), (T-R)/(R-S)\}$, while TFT is subgame perfect
only on the knife-edge $T - R = P - S$ (Theorem 4.9). Simulated matches with
geometric stopping reproduce every threshold.

*Selection.* The replicator dynamics of $\{$ALLC, ALLD, TFT$\}$ is
bistable: ALLD is the unique ESS, TFT is only neutrally stable, and a
population needs a reciprocator share above
$x^* = {{replicator.x_star|.4f}}$ (at $\delta = 0.9$) to escape defection.
The numerical trajectories, the symbolic Jacobian spectra and the fitted
convergence rate all agree. In finite populations a single reciprocator is
nevertheless *favoured* by selection, with fixation probability above
$1/N$ and matching Nowak's exact formula. Mutation erodes cooperation
in the three-strategy world through the ALLC$\to$ALLD$\to$TFT cycle but
not in a rich strategy space, where random mutants are mostly conditional
cooperators. On a lattice, cooperation survives without any memory at all,
with $f_C = {{spatial.coop_1p8_2|.3f}}$ against the published $0.318$, but the
survival at large $b$ is hostage to synchronous updating.

*Noise and adaptation.* Treating memory-one play as a Markov chain gives
exact payoffs under implementation error: TFT's self-play payoff drops
discontinuously to $(T+R+P+S)/4$ for any $\varepsilon>0$, whereas the
generous strategies lose only $O(\varepsilon)$, and selection under noise
tracks exactly that ranking. Zero-determinant extortion pins every
opponent, including a Q-learner, to a line on which the extortioner earns
more, yet it loses tournaments and goes extinct in populations, because it
cannot cooperate with itself, and is driven to extinction or to a small mutation–selection residue.

The through-line is that *which theory applies is a modelling decision*:
the same game predicts defection, cooperation, or coexistence depending on
whether players reason, are selected, tremble, or learn. The laboratory
built for this project makes those decisions explicit parameters, and
every claim above can be regenerated from one command.

## Reproducibility and verification

The accompanying repository contains:

* `src/pdlab/`: a typed Python package (game, strategies, exact Markov
  analysis, symbolic thresholds, replicator and Moran dynamics, spatial
  lattice, Q-learning, tournament engine, experiments, figures, CLI).
* `tests/`: about 350 tests including property-based tests (Hypothesis) of the
  payoff invariants, exact checks of every closed form against simulation,
  and symbolic-versus-numerical checks of the Jacobian spectra; coverage
  is 99% and continuous integration runs the suite, `ruff` and `mypy` on
  every push.
* `results/canonical.json`: every number in this report, regenerated by
  `make reproduce` in about two minutes and substituted into the
  manuscript at build time.
* `results/crosscheck_axelrod.md`: agreement with the independent
  `axelrod` library on all 91 deterministic pairings and on all three
  memory-one vectors.
* `app/index.html`: an interactive, self-contained version of the
  experiments in which every displayed constant is taken from
  `canonical.json`.

Three published values were cross-checked: the Nowak–May cooperator
fraction (0.318; ours {{spatial.coop_1p8_2|.3f}}), the Stewart–Plotkin
zero-determinant vectors (exact agreement), and the Beaufils–Delahaye–Mathieu
result that Gradual outranks TFT in round-robin play (reproduced).
