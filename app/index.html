Finite horizon · backward induction

200rounds still cooperative, of 200

Two hundred rounds against the same opponent, and both of you know it. In the last round nothing follows, so defecting is simply better. Both of you can see that.

So round 199 is the last one where behaviour can still matter. Except that it cannot, because round 200 is already lost. Defect there too.

Every round falls to the one after it, all the way back to the first. The unique subgame-perfect outcome of a known-length game is mutual defection from the opening move.

Take the end away and the arithmetic changes. With a continuation probability δ the future is worth 1/(1−δ) rounds, and patience has a price you can compute.

Noise · memory-one Markov chain

ε = 0.000implementation error per move, swept by scroll

Eight strategies playing themselves, exact long-run payoff from the stationary distribution. At ε = 0 they all sit on R = 3, except the ones that were never nice.

One slip and Tit-for-Tat falls to (T + R + P + S)/4 = 9/4, for any ε > 0: the chain it drives is doubly stochastic. Pavlov and generous TFT lose only a multiple of ε.

The live column on the right is computed here from the 4 × 4 chain at the swept ε; the curves are Python's. They agree to the printed precision.
