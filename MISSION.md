# Mission: Prisoner's Dilemma in Game Theory

## Why
Philip is building a portfolio-grade final project on the Prisoner's Dilemma (rigorous report + simulations + interactive visualization) to showcase in job applications. He needs to understand the math deeply enough to *write, defend and explain it in interviews* — not just present agent-generated work.

## Success looks like
- Prove from scratch that (D,D) is the unique Nash equilibrium of the one-shot PD, and explain why it's a "dilemma" (Pareto).
- Derive the discount-factor threshold δ* for Grim Trigger / Tit-for-Tat in the infinitely repeated PD, and explain the Folk Theorem in one breath.
- Write down the replicator equation for ALLC/ALLD/TFT, find fixed points, and classify their stability.
- Explain Axelrod's tournament results and Nowak–May spatial cooperation to a hiring manager without notes.
- Read Press & Dyson (2012) and explain what a zero-determinant strategy is.

## Constraints
- Time: a couple of days, intense. Lessons must be dense, no filler, fast wins.
- Math background: strong (linear algebra, ODEs, proofs). Skip hand-holding on math; spend effort on game-theoretic concepts.
- Game theory background: none. Start from payoff matrices.
- Parallel track: a separate agent session builds the project; lessons should keep pace with what that project needs.

## Out of scope
- General game theory beyond what PD needs (no Bayesian games, mechanism design, auctions).
- Cooperative game theory (Shapley, core).
