Infinite Repetition, Discounting and Grim Trigger

Lesson 3 · Remove the last round and cooperation becomes an equilibrium — exactly when players are patient enough. Derive the number.

~20 min · Prerequisite: Lesson 2 · Reference: Glossary · Mission: derive δ* for Grim Trigger and justify it with the one-shot deviation principle.

Warm-up (Lesson 2): what did the unraveling proof need from the stage game, and what does SPE add to NE?

A unique stage NE, so the continuation is a constant. SPE demands a NE in every subgame, including off-path ones.

1. The infinitely repeated game

Play the PD in rounds t = 0, 1, 2, … with no last round. Payoff for a stream u0, u1, …:

Discounted payoff U = Σt≥0 δt ut, with discount factor δ ∈ (0,1). Often normalised as (1−δ)U so a constant stream u has value u.

Two readings of δ, mathematically identical: (a) impatience — a unit tomorrow is worth δ today; (b) continuation probability — after each round the game ends with probability 1−δ. Reading (b) is why the "infinite" game is the right model for interactions of uncertain length, which fixes Lesson 2's paradox: no known last round ⇒ nothing to unravel from [Polak, ECON 159, repeated games].

Useful sums (you know these): Σt≥0 δt = 1/(1−δ), Σt≥1 δt = δ/(1−δ).

2. The tool: one-shot deviation principle

Checking SPE naively means checking every strategy against infinitely many deviations at infinitely many histories. This theorem collapses it:

One-shot deviation principle. In a discounted repeated game with bounded stage payoffs, a strategy profile is an SPE iff no player can profit, at any history, by deviating in that round only and conforming to the strategy thereafter [Osborne & Rubinstein, Lemma 98.2 / Prop. 8.x].

Why it holds (sketch): if a profitable multi-round deviation exists, discounting makes its gain from the tail vanish, so truncating to finitely many deviations keeps it profitable; then peel off the last deviating round — if that single deviation isn't profitable, removing it makes the deviation even better, contradiction by induction. Bounded payoffs + δ<1 is what makes the tail vanish ("continuity at infinity").

3. Grim Trigger

Grim Trigger: cooperate in round 0 and at every history in which nobody has ever defected; defect forever after any defection by anyone.

Two kinds of history, so two checks.

(a) On-path: nobody has defected yet

conform (C forever):        R + δR + δ²R + …  =  R/(1−δ)
deviate once (D now, then   T + δP + δ²P + …  =  T + δP/(1−δ)
  Grim says D forever)

No profitable deviation iff R/(1−δ) ≥ T + δP/(1−δ). Multiply by (1−δ): R ≥ (1−δ)T + δP, i.e. δ(T−P) ≥ T−R:

δ ≥ δ*Grim = (T − R)/(T − P)

Canonical payoffs: δ* = (5−3)/(5−1) = 1/2.

(b) Off-path: someone has defected

Grim prescribes D forever for both. Deviating once to C yields S + δP/(1−δ) < P/(1−δ) since S < P. Never profitable, for any δ. (This is just "the stage NE repeated is always an SPE".)

Theorem. (Grim, Grim) is an SPE of the discounted infinitely repeated PD iff δ ≥ (T−R)/(T−P). ∎

Read the formula. Numerator: one-time gain from cheating. Denominator: per-round loss during punishment, scaled. S is absent — the sucker payoff never enters, because Grim never expects to be suckered on-path and never cooperates off-path. Bigger temptation or milder punishment (P close to R) demands more patience.

4. Explore

Interactive — δ* explorer

Edit payoffs (must remain a PD) and slide δ. Tasks:

  1. Confirm δ* = 0.5 for 5,3,1,0. Slide δ across it and watch the two present values cross.
  2. Change S from 0 to −10. Does δ* move? Explain in one sentence.
  3. Set P = 2.9 (just below R). What happens to δ*? Interpret: why is a weak punishment fatal?
  4. Find payoffs where δ* < 0.2 — cooperation "easy". What did you have to do to T?

5. What we have and haven't shown

We showed cooperation can be an equilibrium outcome — not that it must. (ALLD, ALLD) is still an SPE at every δ. Once δ ≥ δ* the game has many SPE; the next lesson shows (Tit-for-Tat) that the threshold depends on the strategy, and that essentially every feasible, individually rational payoff can be sustained: the Folk Theorem. Also note where 2R > T+S finally bites: it guarantees (C,C) forever is the best symmetric outcome — otherwise players would prefer to sustain alternating exploitation instead.

6. Retrieval check

Recall: derive δ* aloud from the two sums, then open.

R/(1−δ) ≥ T + δP/(1−δ) ⇒ R ≥ (1−δ)T + δP ⇒ δ(T−P) ≥ T−R ⇒ δ ≥ (T−R)/(T−P).
